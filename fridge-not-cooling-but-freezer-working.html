Fridge Not Cooling But Freezer Working? Common Causes & Solutions

RepairNow Mumbai Team Refrigerator Repair Tips 7–9 min read

You open the fridge to take out milk or vegetables, but everything feels warmer than it should. Then you check the freezer: the ice is still frozen and the food there seems fine. So what is going on?

A refrigerator that is not cooling properly while the freezer still works can be confusing. In many models, the freezer and fridge compartments share a cooling system, with cold air moving into the refrigerator section through vents and a fan. If that airflow is restricted or a related component is not working correctly, the freezer may stay cold while the fridge compartment becomes warm.

There can be several reasons for this, and the design differs between refrigerator models. Below, we explain the common causes, a few safe checks you can make at home, and when it is time to arrange a technician visit.

Why Is My Fridge Not Cooling But the Freezer Is Working?

In many frost-free refrigerators, the cooling system produces cold air around the freezer evaporator. A fan and air passages help distribute some of that cold air to the fridge compartment. If the airflow is blocked, the fan is not operating, or the air-control system is faulty, the freezer may remain cold while the fridge section struggles to maintain its temperature.

Common causes include:

  1. Blocked refrigerator air vents
  2. Evaporator fan not working properly
  3. Ice buildup caused by a defrost-system problem
  4. Faulty or stuck air damper
  5. Damaged door gasket or a door that does not close properly
  6. Incorrect temperature settings or poor air circulation
  7. Faulty temperature sensor or control-related component

1. Blocked Air Vents Inside the Refrigerator

This is one of the first things worth checking. Cold air needs a clear path to move from one compartment to another. Large containers, food packets or boxes pushed against the rear wall can cover the air outlets and restrict circulation.

What you may notice

  • The freezer seems normal, but the fridge feels warm.
  • Some shelves are colder than others.
  • Food is packed tightly around the rear air outlets.

What you can check

Move food containers away from the visible air vents and leave some space between items so air can circulate. Avoid packing the compartment too tightly. Check the user manual if you are unsure where the vents are located.

2. Evaporator Fan Not Working

The evaporator fan helps move cold air through the refrigerator in many frost-free models. If the fan stops, runs intermittently or becomes obstructed by ice, the freezer may still feel cold while the fridge section receives too little cool air.

Possible signs

  • Airflow from the fridge vents seems weak or absent.
  • You hear an unusual buzzing, scraping or clicking sound.
  • The refrigerator compartment remains warm even after food is rearranged.

Fan operation can vary by model and may pause when the door is open. Do not remove internal panels or try to reach the fan yourself. If airflow remains poor, ask a technician to inspect the fan and its controls.

3. Ice Buildup Due to a Defrost-System Problem

Frost can naturally form around the evaporator during normal operation, and the refrigerator periodically runs a defrost cycle to clear it. If a component in that system fails, ice may accumulate and restrict airflow. This can leave the freezer apparently cold while the fridge section does not receive enough cool air.

Possible signs

  • Visible heavy frost or ice on the freezer's inside rear panel.
  • Cooling becomes weaker over time.
  • The issue returns after a temporary improvement.

A defrost heater, sensor, thermostat, fuse or control fault may be involved, depending on the model. Do not chip away ice with a knife or use a hair dryer or direct heat. A technician should identify the cause before parts are replaced.

4. Faulty or Stuck Air Damper

Some refrigerators use an air damper to regulate how much cold air enters the fridge compartment. If the damper is stuck closed, blocked or not responding to the temperature control, the fridge may not receive enough cold air even though the freezer is operating.

What you can check

Make sure food is not obstructing the visible air outlet. If the vent is clear but the fridge remains warm, the damper may need inspection. Its location and design vary, so avoid trying to force it open.

5. Damaged Door Gasket or Door Not Closing Properly

The rubber gasket around the refrigerator door helps keep cold air inside. If it is torn, dirty, loose or not sealing evenly, warm air can enter and make it harder for the fridge to maintain its temperature. A container or shelf item can also prevent the door from closing fully.

What you can check

  • Look for visible cracks, gaps or loose sections in the gasket.
  • Check that bottles or food packets are not stopping the door from closing.
  • Wipe away dirt from the accessible gasket surface with a soft damp cloth, then dry it.

If the seal is damaged or the door is misaligned, arrange an inspection rather than trying to glue or reshape the gasket yourself.

6. Incorrect Temperature Settings or Poor Air Circulation

Sometimes the settings have been changed accidentally, or the refrigerator is overloaded after a grocery shop. A warm room, frequent door opening and placing a large amount of warm food inside can also affect cooling for a while.

Try these simple checks

  • Check that the fridge is not set to a warmer-than-needed setting.
  • Make sure Holiday, Demo or similar special modes are not enabled, if your model has them.
  • Do not block the air vents or overfill the shelves.
  • Keep the door closed as much as possible while the appliance stabilises.

Use the temperature guidance in your refrigerator's manual. Different models have different controls, so avoid relying on a setting number from another brand or model.

7. Faulty Temperature Sensor or Control Component

A temperature sensor helps the refrigerator monitor conditions inside the compartments. If a sensor gives incorrect readings, or a related control component does not operate correctly, cooling may become inconsistent. This is harder to identify from outside the appliance.

If the vents are clear, the door seals properly and the settings are correct but the fridge remains warm, a technician can check the sensor, wiring and control system. Do not open electrical covers or test live components yourself.

Does a Cold Freezer Mean the Compressor Is Fine?

Not necessarily. A freezer that is still cold is a useful clue, but it does not confirm that every part of the refrigerator's cooling system is working correctly. Airflow, defrost and temperature-control problems can cause different temperatures in the two compartments. A proper diagnosis is better than guessing which part has failed.

What Should You Check Before Booking a Refrigerator Repair?

Before arranging a visit, you can make a few safe observations:

  • Check the fridge temperature setting and any special operating mode.
  • Move food away from visible air vents.
  • Check whether the door closes fully and the gasket looks intact.
  • Look for visible heavy frost on the freezer's rear inside panel.
  • Listen for unusual noises, but do not remove panels to inspect a fan.
  • Note any error code shown on the display and the refrigerator's brand and model.
Important: Do not dismantle the refrigerator, touch internal wiring, chip away ice or apply direct heat. If you notice a burning smell, sparking or another electrical safety concern, stop using the appliance and seek professional assistance.

Food Safety: What to Do While the Fridge Is Warm

If the refrigerator compartment is not staying cold, protect perishable food first. FSSAI guidance recommends keeping high-risk food at or below 5°C. Use a refrigerator thermometer rather than relying only on how cold the shelves feel. Move milk, cooked food, meat, seafood and other perishables to a reliably cold refrigerator or an insulated cooler with ice packs while you arrange a repair.

Do not taste food to decide whether it is safe. If you are unsure how long food has been above a safe temperature, follow food-safety guidance and discard items that may be unsafe.

Does This Problem Always Mean the Fridge Needs Gas Filling?

No. A fridge that is warm while the freezer still works does not automatically mean there is a refrigerant leak or that gas filling is required. Blocked vents, fan issues, frost buildup, a damper problem or a sensor fault may cause similar symptoms. Refrigerant work should only be considered after a qualified technician has checked the appliance and confirmed the actual fault.

When Should You Call a Refrigerator Repair Technician?

Arrange a professional inspection if the fridge remains warm after checking the settings, door closure and visible vents, or if you notice heavy frost, unusual fan noises, repeated temperature changes or an error code. Early diagnosis can help avoid food spoilage and unnecessary part replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my fridge not cooling but the freezer is working?+
In many refrigerators, cold air is circulated from the freezer section into the fridge through vents and a fan. Blocked vents, a fan problem, frost buildup, a faulty air damper or a door-seal issue may affect cooling in the fridge compartment. The exact design varies by model.
Can blocked air vents make the fridge warm?+
Yes. Food containers placed directly in front of the air vents can restrict cold-air circulation. Leave space around the vents and avoid overfilling the refrigerator.
How do I know if the refrigerator fan is not working?+
You may notice weak airflow, unusual noises or a fridge compartment that stays warm. These signs do not confirm a fan fault; a technician may need to inspect the fan and related components.
Can ice buildup cause the fridge section to stop cooling?+
Yes. Excessive frost around the evaporator area can restrict airflow. A defrost-system fault is one possible cause and usually needs professional diagnosis.
Does a freezer that is still cold mean the gas is fine?+
Not necessarily. A working freezer does not by itself confirm the condition of the sealed cooling system. Avoid assuming that gas filling is required without a proper diagnosis.
What temperature should high-risk food be kept at?+
FSSAI guidance says high-risk food should be kept at or below 5°C. Use a refrigerator thermometer and move perishable food to a reliably cold refrigerator or cooler if the compartment is not staying cold.
Should I switch off my fridge if the fridge compartment is warm?+
If the appliance is making unusual electrical noises, smells burnt or shows another safety concern, stop using it and seek professional help. Otherwise, protect perishable food first and arrange an inspection if basic checks do not restore cooling.

Final Thoughts

A fridge that is not cooling while the freezer still works may have an airflow, fan, defrost, damper, door-seal or temperature-control issue. If the problem continues, a technician can inspect the appliance and identify the actual cause.
